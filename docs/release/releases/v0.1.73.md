# Curatorr v0.1.73

## Summary

- **Discover suggestions are now reconciled against your real Lidarr library state** before artist review runs, so manually-added Lidarr artists stop showing up as fresh Curatorr suggestions.
- **Artist progression is more resilient to stale Lidarr references** because Curatorr now keeps `album_acquired` artists in the acquired state when Lidarr still reports downloaded files, even if the tracked album id has gone stale.
- **Discovery candidates are cleaner** because collaboration credits like `feat.`, `featuring`, `ft.`, and known `Artist A & Artist B` joint credits are filtered out of artist suggestions.

## Fixed

- Added a pre-review Lidarr reconciliation step that scans the current Lidarr artist list and marks matching `suggested` entries as `already_in_lidarr`.
- Fixed Discover pipeline noise caused by artists that were added directly in Lidarr outside Curatorr; those artists are now recognised during review instead of being surfaced again as new suggestions.
- Fixed stale tracked-album regressions for artists already at `album_acquired`; if Lidarr still shows downloaded track files for the artist, Curatorr preserves the acquired stage and delays the next review instead of re-queuing another album.
- Improved resilience when Lidarr nightly or metadata refreshes change tracked album ids, avoiding unnecessary stage rollback for already-acquired artists.
- Filtered MusicBrainz-style collaboration credits out of both library-derived and Last.fm-derived artist suggestion pools.
- Prevented known in-library `Artist A & Artist B` collaboration names from being treated as standalone discovery candidates when both credited artists already exist in the catalog.

## Upgrade Notes

- No manual migration is required for this release.
- Existing `suggested` artists that are already present in Lidarr will be reconciled the next time the artist review job runs.
- If you previously saw acquired artists regress after Lidarr changed album ids, those rows should stabilise after the next review cycle.
